What He’s Worth to You

The board price is one number for all twelve seats. It can’t be right for all of them: a manager holding three running backs and a manager holding none are not bidding on the same thing. Build a roster below and watch the room’s price and yours come apart.

A player is worth his points over a bar. The room uses replacement level — the best man nobody has to start. Your bar starts there and rises as you fill slots, because it becomes the man he would have to bench. It is not last-rostered, which sits below replacement and asks a different question: is he worth a bench spot at all?

Running out of room does not by itself make a player worthless. Once your slots are full, anyone over the bar is still worth the upgrade — you start him and bench the man he beat. Anyone under it is worth exactly the $1 floor, because he would sit on your bench and score nothing.
