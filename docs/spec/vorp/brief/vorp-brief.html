00 · Definition

What VORP is

Points don't set a player's price — the last freely-available player at his position does. VORP is the margin above that line, floored at zero.

VORP = max(0, player's points − replacement level at his position)

Replacement level isn't the worst player in the league — it's the best one left once every seat that needs the position has one. Static VORP computes this once, pre-draft, from the full undrafted pool and the league's total per-position demand.

Worked example: 12 tight ends needed league-wide → replacement level is the 13th tight end. A player right at that line is worth 0 VORP; everyone below it is floored at 0 too — nobody's worth less than what's freely available.

01 · Mechanism

Replacement level

Flex slots don't split by a fixed ratio. Solve one league-wide optimal lineup, and replacement level is just the best player at each position the solve didn't pick.

Every team's slots — concrete and flex, pooled across the whole league — get matched against the full player board to maximize total points. Whichever position actually has the better player wins each flex slot, this year, on this board. Everyone left over, position by position, sets replacement level directly.

Assumes optimal drafting, not real drafting — this is what the market should look like, not a forecast.

See 01-calculating-replacement.md for the matching diagram and the exact input/output contract.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ A strong TE class pulls FLEX slots away from RB — replacement level for TE is measured after those claims.
  • ✓ A shallow TE class claims no FLEX at all — replacement level is just the first TE past the concrete slots.
  • ✓ 12 teams, REC_FLEX all claimed by WR → TE replacement level = 140 (the 13th TE), arrived at by solving, not assuming.
  • ✗ A position eligible for no slot at all (e.g. K, this league) is unreachable, not zero — zero means "worth nothing above a real baseline," unreachable means "no baseline exists."
02 · A second baseline

Last rostered

Replacement level asks what a free starter scores. Last-rostered pools bench slots too and asks what the very last player drafted at all scores — a lower, deeper line.

Same league-wide optimal fill as 01, just with bench slots added to the pool (teams × 16 here, vs. teams × 10 for starters only). A bench slot isn't tied to one fixed eligibility list — it accepts anyone at a position the template plays somewhere, concrete or flex.

See 02-value-over-last-rostered.md for the full-roster matching diagram and the exact input/output contract.
Correction · real-data review Bench eligibility is restricted to draftable_positions() — positions the template plays somewhere — not every position unconditionally. Unrestricted, kickers picked up a nonzero last-rostered level even though this league never drafts one.
Correction · calibration DEF is excluded from bench eligibility (STREAMING_POSITIONS) despite being fully draftable at its concrete slot — managers stream defense off waivers rather than stash a second one. DEF's last-rostered level equals its replacement level as a result.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ A WR with no concrete slot, only FLEX-eligible, can still get rostered off the bench if it outscores the alternatives.
  • ✗ A position absent from the template entirely (no concrete slot, no flex eligibility) stays unreachable — bench never resurrects a position, no matter how it projects.
  • ✓ A position with real bench depth (RB) gets a lower last-rostered level than replacement level — more get drafted than the starting-only fill selects.
  • ✗ A DEF projected to outscore everyone still isn't benched beyond one per team — a lower-scoring player at a non-streamed position wins the slot instead.
