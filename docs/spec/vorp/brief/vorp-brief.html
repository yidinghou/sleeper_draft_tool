00 · Definition

What VORP is

Points don't set a player's price — the last freely-available player at his position does. VORP is the margin above that line, floored at zero.

VORP = max(0, player's points − replacement level at his position)

Replacement level isn't the worst player in the league — it's the best one left once every seat that needs the position has one. Static VORP computes this once, pre-draft, from the full undrafted pool and the league's total per-position demand.

Worked example: 12 tight ends needed league-wide → replacement level is the 13th tight end. A player right at that line is worth 0 VORP; everyone below it is floored at 0 too — nobody's worth less than what's freely available.

01 · Mechanism

Replacement level

Flex slots don't split by a fixed ratio. Solve one league-wide optimal lineup, and replacement level is just the best player at each position the solve didn't pick.

Every team's slots — concrete and flex, pooled across the whole league — get matched against the full player board to maximize total points. Whichever position actually has the better player wins each flex slot, this year, on this board. Everyone left over, position by position, sets replacement level directly.

Assumes optimal drafting, not real drafting — this is what the market should look like, not a forecast.

See 01-calculating-replacement.md for the matching diagram and the exact input/output contract.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ A strong TE class pulls FLEX slots away from RB — replacement level for TE is measured after those claims.
  • ✓ A shallow TE class claims no FLEX at all — replacement level is just the first TE past the concrete slots.
  • ✓ 12 teams, REC_FLEX all claimed by WR → TE replacement level = 140 (the 13th TE), arrived at by solving, not assuming.
  • ✗ A position eligible for no slot at all (e.g. K, this league) is unreachable, not zero — zero means "worth nothing above a real baseline," unreachable means "no baseline exists."
02 · A second baseline

Last rostered

Replacement level asks what a free starter scores. Last-rostered pools bench slots too and asks what the very last player drafted at all scores — a lower, deeper line.

Same league-wide optimal fill as 01, just with bench slots added to the pool (teams × 16 here, vs. teams × 10 for starters only). A bench slot isn't tied to one fixed eligibility list — it accepts anyone at a position the template plays somewhere, concrete or flex.

See 02-value-over-last-rostered.md for the full-roster matching diagram and the exact input/output contract.
Correction · real-data review Bench eligibility is restricted to draftable_positions() — positions the template plays somewhere — not every position unconditionally. Unrestricted, kickers picked up a nonzero last-rostered level even though this league never drafts one.
Correction · calibration DEF is excluded from bench eligibility (STREAMING_POSITIONS) despite being fully draftable at its concrete slot — managers stream defense off waivers rather than stash a second one. DEF's last-rostered level equals its replacement level as a result.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ A WR with no concrete slot, only FLEX-eligible, can still get rostered off the bench if it outscores the alternatives.
  • ✗ A position absent from the template entirely (no concrete slot, no flex eligibility) stays unreachable — bench never resurrects a position, no matter how it projects.
  • ✓ A position with real bench depth (RB) gets a lower last-rostered level than replacement level — more get drafted than the starting-only fill selects.
  • ✗ A DEF projected to outscore everyone still isn't benched beyond one per team — a lower-scoring player at a non-streamed position wins the slot instead.
03 · Two lenses

VORP $ and VOLR $

Two whole-budget dollar figures on one scale, and deliberately no single blended bid — the human reads both and decides.

VORP $ apportions the whole budget by VORP alone, among the starters who clear replacement level. VOLR $ apportions the whole budget by value over last rostered, among everyone who clears the last-rostered bar. Each independently sums to teams × budget; a player outside a lens's population is null there, never $0.

Each gets the full budget so the two numbers share a unit and are comparable — the old 90/10 split put them on different scales, and its collapsed bid went non-monotonic at the starter/bench seam (Cam Ward priced above the higher-scoring Bryce Young). Read the gap: VORP $ ≫ VOLR $ is depth-survivable startability, VOLR $ ≫ VORP $ is a cliff to the waiver wire.

See 03-vorp-to-bid.md for the apportionment machinery, floor-pressure, and the exact per-lens contract.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ Two starters, one with double the VORP → his share of the VORP lens is exactly double the other's; same rule for VOLR margin against its lens.
  • ✓ Each lens is monotonic in points within a position — the one property the collapsed blended bid failed.
  • ✗ Neither figure is the price, and the two aren't directly rankable across the board — VOLR's lower bar and larger population lift the middle relative to VORP.
  • ✗ A player with zero margin still gets min_bid, not $0; a position the template never plays (K) is absent from both.
04 · The shipped price

Blended-bar pricing

One price per player out of a single pool — measured against a bar that slides between the replacement and last-rostered ends, monotonic by construction.

Blend the bars, not 03's two prices: bar(p) = w·replacement + (1−w)·last_rostered, with w = w_floor + (1−w_floor)·t and t ramping linearly in points from 0 to 1 over the top FULL_WEIGHT_SHARE (0.75) of starters. Above that point the bar is pure replacement level; below it, a marginal starter and a strong bench pick land on one continuum instead of falling off a cliff. Margins apportion with apportion_with_floor, so the column sums to exactly teams × budget.

Linear in points — not rank — is what keeps price rising with points: it makes d(bar)/d(points) a constant that stays ≤ 1 as long as the ramp spans the gap between its two bars. There is exactly one dial, w_floor, set by a human on the exported slider; FULL_WEIGHT_SHARE is a fixed constant of the model's shape.

See 04-blended-bar-pricing.md for the ramp derivation, the QB flex-peer floor, and the worked TE inversion.
Catch · calibration The ramp is a judgement, not a derivation. FULL_WEIGHT_SHARE = 0.75 and the shipped w_floor = 0.5 were picked to land the starter/bench split near its best on the 2026 board — a different board could put them elsewhere. The shape is provably monotonic at every dial setting; which setting is the part most likely to be wrong, which is why the dial is exposed rather than baked in.
05 · The laws

Principles

A pass/fail matrix of every model against every law a price list must satisfy — models are judged by a harness, not argued about.

A model is any (players, config) → Valuation; a principle is any (model, context) → Finding. Principles receive the model itself, so they can re-run it against a shifted board or a mid-draft state, not just inspect one answer. run() memoizes by board identity so each distinct solve is paid for once.

A law must hold or the output isn't a price list — pass/fail, ten of them. A calibration says the model matches the world — graded, three of them, reported but never enforced, because disagreeing with the market is often the entire point. Market error alone would rank a broken model above a sound one whenever the break cancels out; laws catch the crossing that closeness-to-market can't.

See 05-principles.md for the full list of ten laws and three calibrations and the strawman-passes-8/8 story.

What the scenarios pin down

  • ✓ A price-blending model crosses on the thin position → monotonic fails with the offending pair named.
  • ✓ points_proportional scores 9/10, failing only baseline-not-points — a +50 shift moves 126 prices.
  • ✗ A strawman that passes every law is a finding about the suite, not a compliment to the strawman.
  • ✗ The mid-draft law checks a friendly two-state assumption, not a real draft — strong enough to catch the unpriceable-position collapse, no more.
07 · Residual repricing

Live draft board

The same blended price from 04, re-solved against the players, slots, and money actually left — every sale reprices everyone.

A static list is priced for a room that no longer exists. Three things change and nothing else does: pool = teams·budget − Σ(sale amounts), slots drop by one per sale, and the player pool loses the sold. 01's replacement level and 02's last-rostered level are re-solved over what remains, and progressive_blend is re-run — the model isn't modified, it's handed a smaller league. Prices reconcile to teams × budget − spent.

Early movement is almost all the money pool, not the bars: ten backs sold takes the pool $2400 → $1821 but RB's replacement level holds, so an untouched Ja'Marr Chase reprices $45 → $41. The catch: every reprice assumes the rest of the room will spend rationally — the very assumption the sale you just watched violated. It's a fair-value model re-solved on new information, not a forecast.

See 07-live-draft-board.md. The live server and UI that run on this are documented in the board/ module (../../board/index.md).
08 · One seat's bid

Seat value

The board price corrected by one seat's own roster, then capped by its own budget — what he's worth to me, and the most I may legally say.

The board price is the same for all twelve seats, which is wrong: a fourth back reaches no scoring slot for a seat that has three. So a player is worth the points he adds to that seat's optimal starting lineup, seated against the seat's own players plus a free replacement body at every position: seat_vorp = lineup(bought + [player]) − lineup(bought). Imputing a body (not scoring empty slots) keeps the answer a margin — an empty seat's values equal league VORP exactly, so only a seat that has bought something disagrees with the board.

Dollars use 04's league-wide exchange rate, so seat bid and board price share a unit: bid = clamp(min_bid + floor(rate × seat_vorp), max_bid(seat)), floored not rounded. Value and legality stay separate — a full roster can still value an upgrade, but a seat with no open spots bids 0.

See 08-seat-value.md for the free-agent-pool argument and the worked $25-board / $16-seat example.
Catch · doesn't reconcile Seat values do not reconcile to the pool — 05's reconciles law can't hold when twelve seats each value the same player. This model sits beside 04, never replaces it, and says nothing about what a player will actually cost, which depends on the other eleven seats.
09 · The knapsack

The best affordable roster

The set of players one seat's remaining money can still buy for the most starting-lineup points — the auction's knapsack, solved greedily.

08 prices one player in isolation; this asks about the whole shopping list. It's a knapsack, not a value-per-dollar sort, because the value of a set isn't the sum of its players: a benched player scores nothing and a second elite back can be worth far less than the first. So the objective is 08's lineup solve re-run as the set grows. Greedy by marginal-points-per-dollar, re-valuing against roster-plus-picks-so-far, stopping when the best affordable player adds 0 startable points. max_bid makes feasibility free — a dollar is already reserved for every other open slot.

Greedy is an approximation (exact knapsack is NP-hard): exactly optimal when prices are flat, within a tight bound otherwise. It spends against the current board price (04/07), so the output is "the best roster reachable if prices hold" — feed it live residual prices to keep it honest. exclude_positions drops a streaming position like DEF; fill_all names a cheap body for every leftover slot, tagged so fills never masquerade as upgrades.

See 09-optimal-roster.md for the greedy loop, the optimality bound, and the RosterPlan output shape.
09b · Price luck

Roster scenarios

09 re-run under N seeded price-jitter scenarios, side by side, flagging the "robust buys" that appear in every seed.

One plan tells you nothing about its fragility — whether a target is a buy you'd make in any market or one that only pencils out on this exact price sheet. So 07's board is solved once for a shared base, then per seed jitter_prices scales every price by an independent uniform draw on [1−sigma, 1+sigma] (--sigma 0.15 is ±15%), floored at min_bid. Because the RNG is seeded by the column's own integer and drawn in fixed order, a seed always yields the same sheet — that determinism is the whole reason for naming seeds. 09 plans against each sheet and the columns lay out next to each other.

A robust buy is a value target — not a fill — present in every seed's plan; robust_note intersects the target sets. none is a real finding, not a failure: on a board deep at your open slots, small price moves swap one near-equal substitute for another, so stay flexible. The catch on top of 09's: the jitter is a uniform ±sigma toy of price luck, not a calibrated forecast — real mispricing is correlated and skewed.

See 09b-roster-scenarios.md for the jitter formula, the seed-5 worked example, and the determinism contract.
